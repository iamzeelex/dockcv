# Code of conduct

This is a small project. The rules are short because the situation is simple:
people are here to make a tool better, and anything that makes that harder for
somebody else is out of place.

## What is expected

Argue about the work, not the person. Disagreement is most of what a code review
*is*, and a project where nobody pushes back produces worse software — but the
subject is always the change, never whoever wrote it.

Assume the other person is competent and short of context, because they usually
are. English is a second language for many contributors, including the
maintainer; read past the phrasing to the point being made.

Say what you actually think, once. Repeating a rejected argument louder is not
persuasion, and neither is repeating it in three other threads.

## What is not

Harassment, insults, and demeaning comments about anyone's identity — including
who they are, where they are from, how they work, or what they do not yet know.
Sexual attention or imagery. Publishing anyone's private information. Sustained
disruption of a discussion after being asked to stop.

None of this becomes acceptable because it was phrased as a joke, or because the
technical point underneath it was correct.

## Scope

Issues, pull requests, discussions and any other space this project uses,
including private messages that start from one of them.

## If something goes wrong

Report it to the maintainer through GitHub's private reporting form on the
**Security** tab, which is not only for vulnerabilities and is the most private
channel available here. Reports are read by one person and are not shared
further.

Responses run from a quiet word to a block, and they are proportionate: a bad day
is not a pattern, and a pattern is not a bad day. Whoever reported something is
told what happened.

The maintainer is held to this document too. Where the maintainer is the problem,
say so publicly — there is nobody above them to appeal to, and daylight is the
only remedy a project this size has.
